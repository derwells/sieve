uses the top 20. A unit is a file, function or chunk submitted for scoring. Files the agent created

They do exist at HEAD, so they compete for rank: see the diagnosis.

## Does the batch-size collapse from the earlier single-repository measurement hold?

The sweep did not reproduce it. An earlier measurement, on a 46-file repository (one question), found that putting all 46
units in one request squeezed the spread to 0.12 to 0.68 and pushed known-relevant files to
ranks 20 and 5, while 4 per request spread 0.09 to 0.90 and ranked them 1 and 2. Across
these five asks, nothing like that appears between 1 and 8 units per request:

- The score range changes little. The minimum to maximum stays ≈0.05 to 0.90 at every setting in every repo; the widest
  and narrowest settings differ by ≤0.05 on either end.
- Recall changes on two asks across the sweep. The only recall changes across the sweep are
  repo-A-terser 0.50 → 1.00 at 8 (memory-file.md rises 0.23 → 0.42 as units per
  request rises 2 → 8) and image-fallback 0.25 at 1 → 0.00 at 2 to 8 (shared-image-component.tsx
  0.82 → 0.52). Both involve single-file score changes. The initial interpretation attributed
  these to run-to-run noise of ±0.1 to 0.3; the assessment below explains why
  this sweep does not establish that noise range.
- Cost falls with bigger batches, and wall time generally falls: packshot $0.0507/20.9 s at
  1 unit against $0.0381/4.7 s at 8 (25% cheaper, 4.4× faster), because the question
  overhead is paid once per unit while the state wrapper is paid once per request.

The earlier measurement used a much larger shared state (46 files in one
30k-token request). Between 1 and 8 units, the score ranges stayed similar while
costs fell through 8 units per request. The sweep supports comparing 4 with 8 as a default, but does not
establish that larger batches preserve recall on every ask.
A separate sweep at 16/32/all would help assess the choice before changing it.

## Diagnosis (gate failed)

Observations used in the initial diagnosis (the assessment below revises it):

- Missed ground-truth files have low scores: `content-schema.ts` 0.15 to 0.18, `storefront-content-adapter.ts` 0.21 to 0.22, `recipe-card-stories.tsx`
  0.18 to 0.33, `design-doc.md` 0.29 to 0.44, `memory-file.md` 0.23 to 0.42. Those values barely move
  across four batch sizes, which suggests the previews provide weak evidence of relevance.
- The initial diagnosis attributed misses to the previews (`unit_payload`, first
  40 lines of the file). The later assessment corrects the claim for
  `memory-file.md`:
  - `design-doc.md` is 1256 lines; the shown head is YAML front matter of colour tokens and
    type scales. The card, the pack shot and the cream background are further down.
  - the content schema (135 lines) shows a module docstring about a validation
    schema and a legacy snapshot; no image field is visible.
  - the storefront content adapter (82 lines shown) is imports plus a single image-schema line.
  - `memory-file.md` opens with an 11-line HTML comment telling the operator not to let the
    assistant edit it; the reply-length facts come later.
  - `agent-instructions.md` (repo-C) opens with "What this project is"; the stale status
    claims are further in. It scored 0.49 to 0.52 at every batch size: right on the
    threshold.
- In the 1090-file repo-B many files have similar high scores. Counting the full
  distribution at 4 units per request: image-fallback has 11 files ≥0.80, 35 ≥0.70,
  71 ≥0.50 for 4 ground-truth files; packshot has 7 ≥0.80, 12 ≥0.70, 44 ≥0.50 for 5.
  The top 10 includes related files: another pack-shot component 0.88/0.91,
  a product lockup component 0.86, and a product page 0.84. A developer might
  open any of these.
- Files created *by the fix itself* rank high at HEAD and take top-10 slots that were not
  available when the ask was made: `fallback-image-component.tsx` 0.83 (rank 5) and
  `storefront-fallback-image-component.tsx` 0.82 (rank 7) in ask 3; `invoice-parser.py` 0.84 in
  ask 5. Two of ten slots in ask 3 are occupied by files that did not exist.
- Three of five asks have 2 ground-truth files, so one miss caps recall@10 at 0.50 and the
  0.8 gate is effectively "retrieve everything". The initial write-up claimed that 5 of 5 asks passed a looser check for the
  primary fix file in the top 10, but its own ranks contradict that claim: `system-prompt-file.md` 1,
  `product-card-component.tsx` 2, `recipe-card-component.tsx`/`shared-image-component.tsx` 19/60 (ask 3 is the exception),
  `readme.md` 1, `costs-workbook-builder.py` 1.

Three hypotheses, ranked:

1. The 40-line preview is the wrong evidence for large or front-loaded files.
   `FILE_PREVIEW_LINES = 40` shows front matter, imports and docstrings. The initial hypothesis was that every
   ground-truth file scoring under 0.35 had relevant content below line 40,
   with stable scores across batch sizes. The later assessment identifies an exception. This suggests a preview problem,
   but does not rule out batch effects. A low-cost test would be to
   expand the preview, or add a path-and-size-aware second look (head + tail, or a
   keyword-selected window) for files above some length. `design-doc.md` at 1256 lines is the
   clearest example: it is the design doc for the ask and scored 0.44.
2. Similar scores among related files in a large repo. The criteria ask "would a
   developer have to open it", and in a 1090-file design-heavy front end 35 files
   legitimately qualify for "an image failed to load". Within the 0.70 to 0.88 band the order
   does not clearly distinguish ~30 plausible candidates, which limits recall@10.
   This is a ceiling that a sharper prompt might raise (ask which file *defines* the
   shared image component rather than which renders an image) but recall@10 over 1090
   files remains sensitive to their ordering.
3. The ground truth includes ripple edits and the repo contains the fix's own new
   files. `*.stories.tsx` files (0.18 to 0.21) were edited because a prop changed;
   `content-schema.ts` and `storefront-content-adapter.ts` were edited because a new image field was threaded
   through. The ask gives little direct evidence for ranking those top-10, and the eval is run at
   HEAD, where the fix's new files outrank the pre-existing ones they replaced. The estimated effect is roughly one to two slots per repo-B ask.

This run left the scoring prompt, `UNITS_PER_REQUEST`, `FILE_PREVIEW_LINES` and
the threshold unchanged. All four remained implementation decisions.

## What changed

Measurement (diagnosis 3). Each ask is now scored against a snapshot of the
*parent* of the commit that answered it, produced with
`git archive <commit>^ | tar -x -C <tmpdir>` into a temporary directory that is
deleted after the run. This did not create a checkout or worktree, touch an
index, or write to the four repositories. The fix's own new files are absent, so they cannot
take a top-10 slot that did not exist at ask time. `created` files stay out of the
denominator as before.

| ask | files walked (HEAD → parent) |
|---|---|
| repo-A-terser | 101 → 94 |
| repo-B-packshot | 1090 → 1080 |
| repo-B-image-fallback | 1090 → 1076 |
| repo-C-status | 62 → 62 |
| repo-D-cost-loaders | 199 → 190 |

Every ground-truth file is present in its snapshot; no `created` file is.

Evidence (diagnosis 1). A file unit's `content` is no longer only the opening
lines. A file longer than 80 lines now gets its first 40 lines, then a
line-numbered outline of the whole file: every heading for Markdown, every
top-level declaration (functions, classes, types, exported consts) for a file with
a tree-sitter grammar, and evenly spaced sample lines for anything else. Outlines
longer than 60 entries are subsampled evenly rather than cut off, so the outline
still reaches the end of the file. The whole preview is capped at
`FILE_PREVIEW_CHARS = 6_000`: the old 40-line head ran ~1.5 to 2.5 kB, so this is
roughly 2 to 3× it, about 1.5k tokens, leaving 8 units plus their questions near 12k
tokens against the 30k state limit. The payload also carries `size_lines`, the
file's total length, and `UNIT_SPEC` says `content` is "its opening lines followed
by a line-numbered outline of the rest". Nothing else in the prompt changed.

Preview text across the four repos grew 1.45× in characters (repo-B 1,879 kB →
2,736 kB), which accounts for the additional cost below.

## Assessment

The rerun improved retrieval but did not pass the gate. Files with relevant
content below line 40 moved up: `design-doc.md` went from rank 70 to 10, and
`agent-instructions.md` from 20 to 3. The parent snapshot also removed two
post-fix files from the image-fallback top-10 candidates. Because both changes
were applied together, these results do not isolate the effect of the outline.

Several missed files required secondary edits: `content-schema.ts` (0.17) and
`storefront-content-adapter.ts` (0.19) needed a new image field passed through a
schema; `recipe-card-stories.tsx` (0.47) needed a prop change;
`article-body-component.tsx` (0.33) also renders an image. The asks do not name
these files directly. This may explain their low ranks, but it does not establish
that a retriever cannot find them. The ground truth measures files touched by
the change, which may differ from the files an agent needs to read first.

The initial diagnosis was wrong about `memory-file.md`. It is 47 lines and was
already shown whole in run 1, so its 0.22 to 0.38 scores cannot be explained by a
truncated preview. Jev scored this file of stored facts as weakly relevant to
"make the bot terser". Whether that judgment is useful requires a separate review.

Three of five asks have only two ground-truth files. One miss caps recall@10 at
0.50, so the 0.8 gate requires retrieving every ground-truth file for those asks.
A looser, post-hoc check asks whether the primary fix file appears in the top 10.
The rerun is 5/5 on that check (`system-prompt-file.md` 1,
`product-card-component.tsx` 1, `shared-image-component.tsx` 2, `readme.md` 1,
`costs-workbook-builder.py` 1). This is a separate observation, not a pass on the
original gate or a measurement of coding-agent performance.

Scores also vary. On identical snapshots and previews,
`recipe-card-component.tsx` scored 0.82 at 4 units per request, 0.34 at 8 and
0.64 at 16. `memory-file.md` scored between 0.22 and 0.42 across seven runs.
These measurements change batch size as well as run, so they do not establish a
noise floor. The earlier claim that differences under ~0.15, or single-cell
recall differences with a 2-file denominator, carry no information is not
supported by repeated measurements at fixed settings. The larger rank changes
are worth investigating, but their stability needs further measurement.

Decision: keep the current criteria. All four sweep runs sum to 5.00 on relaxed recall@10. Open has mean primary probability 0.838, below control 1 at 0.846 and equal to control 2 at 0.838; its strict recall@10 sum of 2.95 is below both controls. Change has mean primary probability 0.802 and strict recall@10 sum 3.45, below control 1 and equal to control 2. Neither meets the decision rule. `sieve/grep.py` was not changed.

The retained 25 runs cost $0.677365 in total. An initial version of the two alternative prompts accidentally omitted the sentence about judging the whole file. Those ten runs cost $0.232895 and were replaced before comparison. Total API cost for this extension was $0.910260. The main result is narrow: the primary files are consistently easy to find, while secondary edited files still reduce strict recall, especially in the large front end repository. A two run control shows that a single strict recall difference of 0.20 can arise without any prompt change; this sweep is too small to support a broader noise estimate.
